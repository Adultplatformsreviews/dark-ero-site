What Is RedQuill?

RedQuill is a website that writes adult fiction from a prompt. You describe a scenario, answer a few questions, and it streams a chapter you can continue, edit or branch.

A heat control runs from a slow burn to explicit. New accounts get a short tour that says every story is generated fresh, that you steer each chapter, and that the heat slider is yours to set.

The operator in the terms and the footer is Innerworlds, Inc., at a Madison Avenue suite in New York. The privacy policy header still says RedQuill, Inc.

That is a naming mismatch worth noticing. It is not, on its own, a sign the company is fake.

The product still wears a beta label.

Around the editor there is a discover feed, reusable components for tropes and styles, character and scenario tools, roleplay messages, image generation and text-to-speech. The pricing page advertises more than 60 million chapters, 700,000 users and 275,000 components.

Those are the company's own unaudited numbers. Treat them as marketing that happens to match a busy feed, not as an audit.

Dark-Ero files this kind of tool on the AI tools category as well, because the meter and the model are the same questions we ask of the chat apps. The reading experience is the part that belongs with fiction.

What a Quill Buys

Quills are the credit. Almost every AI action spends them, and the plan sets the daily batch.

Daily Quills reset and do not roll into the next period. The terms are explicit about that.

Signup bonuses and promo Quills stay in the balance until you spend them, according to the pricing FAQ.

A chapter costs about 5 Quills. That is the useful arithmetic.

Basic's 80 daily Quills are about 16 chapters. Pro's 150 are about 30.

Premium's 300 are about 60. Roleplay messages, images, paragraph edits and audio draw on the same day.

A long sitting can hit the wall in the middle of a scene. That interruption is the complaint that shows up most often in other people's notes, and the math explains it without needing a lab.

You can earn a few Quills without paying. A hunt inside the app has paid a small batch for social actions such as bookmarking, commenting, following and saving.

Contests pay for taking part. A referral has been described as 500 Quills when a friend subscribes, with a discount on their first purchase.

One-off top-ups exist if you do not want a higher plan. New accounts have also been shown a countdown discount, so the first price on screen may be a promo rather than the rate on the pricing page.

Basic, Pro and Premium

These figures are from RedQuill's pricing page on 2 September 2026. Basic is $13 a month, or $8.50 a month when you pay $102 for the year, with 80 Quills a day, 40 roleplay messages, 40 images, 40 edits, 1,000 audio words and 150 bonus Quills at signup.

Pro is $15, or $10 a month on a $120 year, with 150 Quills, 75 messages, 75 images, 75 edits, 2,000 audio words and 450 bonus Quills. Premium is $27, or about $17.83 a month on a $214 year, with 300 Quills, 150 messages, 150 images, 150 edits, 4,000 audio words and 700 bonus Quills.

Annual billing is advertised at 34% off.

Advanced model uses are capped by the week: 30 on Basic, 50 on Pro, 100 on Premium. Premium is the plan that can download a story for offline reading.

You can cancel in the billing settings, and access lasts until the period ends. Other sites quote nearby prices such as $12.69 or $26.69, often from an old promo or another country.

Use the live checkout.

The free tier is real and small. The paid pricing page we read did not print the free daily number.

Third-party notes cluster around 15 Quills, about three chapters. Confirm that balance in the account rather than treating 15 as an official promise.

Free is enough to see the components and the reader. It is not enough for a regular serial.

Refunds are tight. The terms allow one within 24 hours of payment, and only if you have not used any credits.

A chargeback cancels the subscription and revokes what is left, so write to the care team before you dispute the card. The service is provided as-is, with no promise of quality or uptime, and liability is capped at the greater of $100 or what you paid.

Disputes go to individual arbitration, with a class-action waiver.

Who Owns the Story You Publish

You keep the copyright in what you write and prompt. That is the reassuring sentence.

The next sentence is the purchase. By posting content you grant RedQuill a perpetual, worldwide, irrevocable, royalty-free, sublicensable licence to host, store, display, reproduce, modify and make derivative works from your content and your prompts.

The licence survives if you delete the account.

Publishing goes further. The terms say that, by default, a published story can be viewed and remixed, and that you grant every other user a perpetual, irrevocable licence to reuse it and build on it.

A private story is different. RedQuill says it will use commercially reasonable measures to keep a private story confidential from other users, while keeping its own rights under the terms and the privacy policy.

If you wanted a draft that nobody else can continue, leave it private and do not hit publish.

This is not legal advice. It is the practical reading of the current terms, which were dated December 2024 when we went through them.

If you are writing something you might want to sell elsewhere, the permanent licence and the remix default are the clauses to read twice before the story leaves your account.

Private by Default, and What That Omits

The pricing FAQ says content is private by default, stored with ordinary encryption, and that personal data and stories are not shared with third parties without explicit consent. The privacy policy, last updated 11 April 2024, is older than the terms and less cozy.

It collects your username, the text and image prompts you enter, public chats, IP address, usage data, cookies, email and billing details. Card numbers stay with a payment processor.

RedQuill says it stores a confirmation, not the card.

The policy also covers data collected through training RedQuill's own models, and it lists your prompts among the data the service may collect. It does not clearly say that every private story is fed into training, and it does not clearly say the opposite.

We will not invent a yes or a no. Treat a prompt as something the company can read and may use to improve the product.

The policy allows sharing with service providers, in a sale of the business, with law enforcement, and, in the California sense of the word, sharing of online identifiers with advertising partners, with an opt-out. It says no storage method is perfectly secure.

"Private by default" means other users do not see the draft. It does not mean the draft is invisible to the company.

What the Rules Actually Ban

The guidelines allow a wide range of adult fiction and a lot of kink. They are not a blank page.

Banned categories include minors in a sexual context, including under-18 characters and drawn styles that sexualise children, which the terms treat as grounds for an immediate ban. Also out: sexual content of a real, identifiable person without permission, rape and domestic abuse shown approvingly, serious harm in a ravishment scene, bestiality, graphic violence and human death, sexualised killing, hate, and a short list of other extreme categories.

That is the honest frame. The product drops the everyday refusal a mainstream model gives to consensual adult fiction.

It does not drop the legal lines, and the minor line is not ambiguous. Complaints are reviewed within seven business days, with outcomes from removal to a closed account, and there is an appeal.

The library is user-made, so taste and quality vary. Moderation is allowed.

It is not promised as a complete pre-screen of every chapter.

One test of the guided flow produced adult taboo fiction, including step-family scenarios between adult characters, while the minor ban stayed in the rules. If that theme is not what you want, the heat slider and the prompt are how you steer away from it.

Do not use either control to test the minor ban. The ban is the rule.

RedQuill Scorecard

The product does the job it names. You can go from a one-line idea to a chapter, keep steering, and stay inside adult fiction without a mainstream model slamming the door.

The price table is public, the daily math is understandable, and the company has an address. Components and a community feed are genuine extras if you like tropes and other people's setups.

The meter is the tax, and the licence is the surprise. Sixteen chapters on Basic is a real evening until it is not, published work becomes raw material for other users, and the privacy FAQ is friendlier than the privacy policy.

The 24-hour refund is gone the moment you spend a Quill, which means the free tier is the only low-stakes test.

Put together, that is 6.9 out of 10. Use the free balance on one story you actually want.

Pay for Pro, monthly, only if you finish chapters often enough to hit the cap. Leave anything personal unpublished, and leave anything sensitive out of the prompt.

RedQuill Verdict

RedQuill is a legitimate erotica generator with a community around it and a credit system you can do arithmetic on. It suits a regular reader who will use a daily allowance and who will keep drafts private.

It does not suit someone who wants unmetered output, or a file that cannot be remixed, or a companion that remembers a relationship.

Our score is 6.9 out of 10. Try the free tier, watch how fast the Quills move in one sitting, and only then decide whether $10 a month on the annual Pro plan is a habit you actually have.

Publish nothing you would mind another person continuing.
