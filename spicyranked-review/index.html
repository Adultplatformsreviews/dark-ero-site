How SpicyRanked Reviews Work

The editorial policy describes a four-step review path used across categories. First, reviewers either use a platform on free or paid accounts or research primary sources such as pricing pages, terms, privacy policies and app store listings.

Second, every review is supposed to keep a clear line between what a platform claims, what the reviewer observed and what public evidence supports. SpicyRanked says user counts on a homepage stay the platform's claim until public evidence backs them up.

Third, the reviewer scores six weighted categories from 1 to 10 and publishes a weighted average Heat Score. Fourth, a second team member who did not write the piece edits the score math and claims before publication.

SpicyRanked states it does not use a fixed testing period. Where a review is based on the team's own use, the review is supposed to say so, and any demo or free press account is supposed to be disclosed in that review.

Heat Score Explained

Every platform is scored 1 to 10 across six categories with fixed weights. SpicyRanked publishes those weights on About and in its FAQ: Content Quality 20%, Value for Money 20%, Privacy & Safety 20%, User Interface 15%, Features 15% and Mobile Experience 10%.

The published rating is a weighted average shown as a decimal out of 10. SpicyRanked says the number is calculated from the formula and is not negotiated or adjusted after the fact.

Privacy & Safety scoring covers age verification, moderation, payment security, data handling and breach history. SpicyRanked states that a documented breach in the past 24 months costs at least two points and is disclosed in the review.

Mobile Experience is only 10% of the score, which matters because many adult platforms have no native app. SpicyRanked says mobile web performance then carries more weight than it would in mainstream software.
