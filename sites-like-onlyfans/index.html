What to Compare Before You Pick a Platform

The headline percentage is the first thing creators check, and it matters. Moving from an 80% share to a 90% share is a 12.5% raise on the same work.

Payout rules are the second filter. Look at the minimum withdrawal, the holding period on new earnings and which payout methods work in your country.

Content rules come next. Some sites welcome explicit work, some only allow specific fetish content and some ban nudity entirely.

Discovery is the factor people underrate most. A platform with a browsable marketplace or search can bring in buyers you never had to promote to yourself.

How to Choose the Right Platform for You

Start with your content type, because it rules out half the list straight away. Explicit creators can skip Passes, Patreon and Ko-fi, while SFW creators can skip most of the rest.

Next, decide whether you want subscribers or buyers. Subscription sites reward steady posting, while marketplaces reward a strong catalogue and good tags.

Then check payout access in your country. A great keep rate is useless if the site cannot send money to your bank or preferred wallet.

Look at how much promotion you can realistically do each week. If the answer is very little, favour platforms with search, feeds or a storefront that does some of that work.

Finally, test before you commit. Open a profile, upload a small set of content and track results for a month before you move your whole audience.

Common Mistakes New Creators Make

Many new creators price too low out of fear. Cheap prices attract bargain hunters and make it harder to raise rates later.

Another mistake is posting without a schedule. Fans stay subscribed when they know fresh content is coming on predictable days.

Some creators also ignore their inbox, even though messages often bring in more than subscriptions. Paid messages and custom requests deserve a set time each day.

Lastly, people forget about taxes. Set aside part of every payout, because platform income is usually treated as self-employed earnings.
