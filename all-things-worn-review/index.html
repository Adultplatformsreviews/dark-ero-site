What Is All Things Worn?

All Things Worn is a marketplace for used panties, shoes, socks, lingerie, photo sets, video clips and experiences. Buyers join free. Sellers pay for Premium tools before the full shop opens. The operator named for the business is ATW Group Ltd in Westcliff-on-Sea, United Kingdom.

On 10 November 2026 the homepage claimed 3 million members, $10 million paid to sellers and 1 million items sold. Those are the company's figures, not a count we audited. The same day, a marketing page still mentioned a $15.99 price. We are following the official FAQ, not that stray line.

KinkCoins are an optional on-site balance. Sellers can also agree another payment method with the buyer. Off-platform deals take the site out of the middle, which is exactly when the terms matter, because the company says it may not step into a dispute between you.

The $19.99 Month and the Zero Commission

The FAQ, checked 10 November 2026, lists Premium at about $19.99 a month, auto-renewing every 30 days, or $69.99 as a one-time six-month payment described as two months free. That package is what unlocks contacting buyers, unlimited messages, unlimited listings and the rest of the seller tools. Buyers do not pay a signup fee to browse.

The site takes no commission on sales. You keep the sale amount before cash-out fees. The monthly plan keeps billing until you cancel it. The six-month plan is a one-off. The terms, last updated 10 September 2026, say there are no refunds for subscriptions or KinkCoin purchases, with the usual nod to whatever the law still requires.

A seller paying about $19.99 a month needs the sales to clear that fee before the zero commission means anything. Two small sales can cover it. One quiet month does not. The membership is a business cost, not a tip you get back if the listing sits there.

KinkCoins, Holds and the Gift-Card Cut

Each KinkCoin is worth 10 cents, so 300 coins are $30. Cashing out requires identity verification and a minimum of $30, or $50 for ACH and SEPA. After you pick a method, the site starts the payment after a 7-day hold, and transfers go out with the admin run on Mondays.

Methods on the help page include Amazon gift cards, with a 20 percent service fee, plus direct bank transfer, Paxum and YourSafe. An older blog post still on the site talks about YourSafe under another name and describes one hop as free. We did not find a current official flat fee of $5 or €0.75 to put in this paragraph, so we are not inventing one. Confirm the method you will actually use.

The homepage advertises automated prepaid shipping labels. The terms still say buyers and sellers arrange their own payment and delivery, and that the company does not get involved in their disputes. Use tracked, discreet shipping either way. Do not ship from home.
