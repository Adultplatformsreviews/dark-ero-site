What Is Private Sugar Club?

Private Sugar Club is a sugar-dating platform run from London by SSG Finance Ltd. UK Companies House lists that company as active, number 10863409, incorporated on 12 July 2017, with a registered office at 167 to 169 Great Portland Street, London, W1W 5PF.

The website uses the same address. It is aimed at a global audience and operated as a website, not a native app.

Sugar babies pay a flat membership. Sugar daddies join without a subscription and pay per feature.

Both baby plans are described as including a verified badge, messaging with verified members, the ability to receive payments, content uploads and a 0 percent platform commission on earnings. Prices are shown in dollars and may appear in your local currency at checkout.

Mandatory ID verification is the feature the site wants to be known for. A real company plus a real ID check is a better start than an anonymous board.

It does not mean you will find an arrangement, that every member behaves, or that anyone earns the figure in the advertisement.

Baby Plans You Can Read, Daddy Prices You Cannot

Sugar babies pay $9.99 for three months or $14.99 for six months. Both auto-renew.

The $9.99 plan renews every three months and the $14.99 plan every six. The platform says it emails a reminder before renewal.

That is a low test if verification and sugar dating are actually what you want. It is still a subscription, and a reminder email is easy to miss.

The platform says babies keep 100 percent of tips, allowances, content purchases and direct payments made through the site, and that the membership is its only charge to them. Zero percent is about those payments, not about every fee a bank might add when money leaves a wallet.

Public pages did not clearly list a withdrawal minimum, bank-transfer timing, or every banking fee. We can repeat the commission claim.

We cannot price the cash-out from the pages we read.

Sugar daddies do not buy that membership. The public pricing page lists charges for messaging, tips, gallery and content unlocks, and allowances, and it offers a wallet you can top up in amounts such as $10, $20, $30, $50, $75 and $100.

What we could not confirm is a full price sheet: the cost per message, the fee to start a conversation, typical unlock prices, any platform fee on allowances, or spending limits. Those may appear only after the account exists.

The site advertises average sugar-baby income of about $2,000 to $5,000 a month. That is a platform figure, not a wage we measured and not a promise.

Accounts terminated for breaking the terms are not automatically refunded. Membership billing runs through Segpay, which also has its own billing-support route.

There is no install and no app-store rating history, because there is no native app.

ID Checks, and the Claims We Will Not Repeat

Verification is mandatory, which is stricter than a free dating profile that never sees a document. The site also flags conversation patterns tied to common scams: gift cards, cryptocurrency, wires, a so-called verification fee, and a rush to Telegram or WhatsApp.

Those warnings are useful. They are not a force field.

The privacy policy says the company may process your name or nickname, email, profile, messages, gallery photos, identity documents, activity, membership and transaction details, IP, device and cookie data, and support messages. You are uploading a government ID to a dating site.

Read that sentence before you call the plan cheap.

Trustpilot, at a September 2026 check, showed 4.5 out of 5 from 104 reviews, about 78 percent five-star and 11 percent one-star, with replies to negative feedback and no recent history of the company soliciting reviews. That is a healthier public footprint than most sugar sites.

It is still 104 reviews, not a census.

We will not repeat claims of no scammers, guaranteed members, or a monthly income as facts. A named company can still host a bad chat.

Leave if anyone asks you to pay them off-platform to prove you are real.
