4. FootLove: Best for PayPal Payouts

FootLove sells through coins: buyers buy packs, spend them on your content, and you cash the coins out. Its earnings calculator valued each coin at $0.10 at payout when we checked.

The calculator showed a $4.99 monthly Basic tier with a 70% seller share and a $9.99 Pro tier with an 85% share. The jump to Pro pays for itself fairly quickly once sales are steady.

Cash-outs are sent to PayPal after you collect 500 coins, about $50 in value. FootLove says it completes payouts within 30 days, so expect patience rather than speed.

The site pushes face-free selling under a creator name, which suits privacy-minded sellers. Our FootLove review explains the coin rules in more depth.

5. Footly: Best Low-Fee Newcomer

Footly launched in December 2025, by its own account, and runs from tryfootly.com. It pairs a paid plan with a small per-sale cut that shrinks as you upgrade.

Plans are Rising at $3.99 a month, Spotlight at $6.99 and Icon at $9.99, with platform fees of 15%, 10% and 5% respectively. Yearly versions cost $24.99, $39.99 and $59.99.

Footly says it pays weekly with a $10 minimum, using ACH in the US and Paxum elsewhere. It states that PayPal is not supported.

Seller ID checks run through Ondato and payments through CCBill, according to the company, though we have not tested either. Our Footly review rates it 6.0 out of 10, mainly because its track record is short.

What Kind of Feet Content Sells

Buyers on these sites tend to have specific tastes, so a clear style sells better than random snapshots. Common themes include soles, arches, pedicures, socks, nylons, heels and lotion sets.

Pick two or three themes you can shoot well and repeat them with small variations. Regular buyers like knowing what they will get from you.

Short clips often sell for more than single photos, and custom shoots can sell for more again. Worn items are a separate market that fits All Things Worn or FetishFinder's store better than photo-only sites.

Whatever you sell, write accurate titles and tags. Buyers on marketplaces search by keyword, and vague listings simply do not get seen.

Getting Your First Sale

A platform only gets buyers to your page, and your profile must close the deal. Fill your gallery with a dozen or more sets before you share the link anywhere.

Shoot in soft daylight, keep backgrounds clean and vary angles, poses and props. Variety gives returning buyers a reason to unlock more.

Post on a steady schedule, because both marketplaces and subscription apps favour active accounts. Long gaps often mean lost subscribers.

Custom requests usually earn the most per buyer, so list your limits and prices clearly. Extra ideas are in this guide on turning feet photos into income.

Staying Safe and Avoiding Scams

The classic scam is a buyer who wants content first and payment later. Another is a request to pay by gift card or an app outside the platform.

Keep every sale inside the site's own checkout, where seller protections and records live. Walk away from anyone who pushes otherwise.

Protect your identity with a separate email and creator name. Strip location data from files and keep tattoos, jewellery and room details out of frame.
