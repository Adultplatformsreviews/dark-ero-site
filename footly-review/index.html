Our short verdict on Footly

Footly has some of the clearest fee tiers we have seen on a young feet marketplace. Its catch is simple: you pay before you can list a single photo.

We rate it a cautious option rather than a default pick. It rewards sellers with an audience, and it punishes anyone hoping a marketplace feed will find buyers for them.

  • Works for: creators with social followers who want a low per-sale cut.
  • Weak for: newcomers wanting to trial a site at zero cost.
  • Buyer cost: free sign-up, pay per unlock.
  • Seller cost: a plan fee plus 5–15% of each sale.

How selling on Footly works

The path runs in four steps: create an account, pick a plan, pass ID checks, then build a profile and post. Buyers find creators in a discovery feed and pay through subscriptions, locked posts, tips or custom orders.

Footly bans AI-generated creator content and says uploads are reviewed before publishing. We could not observe that review queue ourselves.

Right after sign-up, the plan picker takes over and locks the feed, inbox, dashboard and uploads. Only settings such as tax info, message rules and custom-request options stay open until you pay.

Messages and custom requests

Message controls are a real highlight. You can limit who contacts you, toggle read receipts and require a tip before any chat starts.

That tip gate screens out time-wasters and turns idle chatter into income. Automated DMs, though, seem to be reserved for Spotlight and Icon.

Custom requests come switched on with a five-day delivery window in settings. The terms reportedly give seven business days instead, so set your own window clearly to avoid mixed expectations.

Footly plans and platform fees

There are three seller tiers, and the app shows the same prices as the public pricing page. A cheaper plan simply means Footly keeps a bigger share of each sale.

Rising: $3.99 a month

Rising costs $3.99 monthly or $24.99 for a year and keeps a 15% cut. You take home 85% of every sale, and Footly aims it at new creators.

Spotlight: $6.99 a month

Spotlight runs $6.99 monthly or $39.99 yearly with a 10% cut. It reportedly unlocks Discovery Boost, mass messages, auto DMs and post scheduling.

Icon: $9.99 a month

Icon is $9.99 monthly or $59.99 yearly and takes only 5%. It reportedly adds a badge and priority placement, which hints that cheaper tiers get less visibility.

You can switch plans yourself from settings without a penalty, according to Footly. That helps when income swings from one month to the next.

Footly fee math by sales level

Take Footly's own worked example of $1,000 in monthly sales: Rising nets $846.01, Spotlight $893.01 and Icon $940.01. We rechecked it, and the plan fee plus the percentage cut produce those exact figures.

Lower volumes tell a different story. At $60 a month, all three monthly plans leave you $47.01, so that is the break-even point.

Under $60, Rising wins because the fixed plan price dominates. Over $60, each upgrade pays off, since every five-point fee cut beats the extra $3 in subscription cost.

Footly itself suggests Icon only above roughly $300 a month. Our sums put the switch far lower, so its advice looks conservative.

Footly payouts: timing, minimum and methods

Footly says it pays creators weekly once the balance hits $10. US sellers get ACH deposits, and everyone else is paid through Paxum.

The pricing page promises one to three business days of processing and says Footly covers payout fees. A seven-day hold on fresh earnings is reportedly in place, and the seller agreement mentions possible reserves.

We did not complete a real withdrawal. Until more sellers report steady payments, treat the timing claims as unproven.

ID verification and sign-up

Creator age and identity checks run through Ondato, a third-party verifier, says Footly. It is included on every plan and is billed as taking minutes.

The test account never reached the Ondato screen before the paywall. Expect a government ID plus a selfie, but confirm the order of steps on screen.

Sign-up asked for basic details and skipped email confirmation. That is fast, yet it makes recovery harder if an address is mistyped.
