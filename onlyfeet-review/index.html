What Is OnlyFeet?

OnlyFeet is a feet-only marketplace. The operator named in the footer is LORDLY, in Paris.

Card payments run through processors that show up on a statement under the shop's billing names. Sellers list photos and videos one item at a time, set a price, and can also take subscriptions and tips.

There is no native phone app, so the whole job is a browser.

The storefront uses a .us.com address. A similarly spelled .com page has been an empty placeholder.

Confirm you are on the shop that names LORDLY before you type a card. We are not linking the address here.

The name in the footer is the check.

The company does process payouts for sellers who reach a threshold, which is the difference between a fiction and a bad deal. Public trust signals are the bad deal.

When we looked at the June 2026 record, Trustpilot had put a breach-of-guidelines warning on the profile, turned the public score off, and said fake reviews had been removed. The homepage was still advertising a self-hosted 4.5 from 297 reviews.

A number the shop gives itself, next to a warning from the review site, is not a rating.

20 Percent, Unless You Buy the Expensive Plan

The homepage says that on Pro you keep up to 100 percent of what buyers pay. The seller agreement is the document that prices everyone else.

OnlyFeet charges a service fee of 20 percent of buyer payments, and Pro subscribers get that fee reduced to 0 percent. Basic pays both the plan and the 20 percent.

The platform fee is charged up front whether or not you sell anything. On Basic it is $9.99 for 7 days, $19.97 for 90 days, or $39 lifetime.

On Pro it is $14.99, $29.97 or $79. A 90-day Basic plan is about $6.66 a month.

On $100 of sales that month you still lose $20 to the service fee, so the all-in bite is roughly 27 percent. On $50 of sales it is about a third.

Pay $9.99 every week and four weeks of fees are $39.96 before the 20 percent. That is a miserable first month, which is exactly the month beginners have.

Payout timing is told three ways. One line says daily.

Another says bank transfers take 5 to 7 business days. A featured testimonial says 30 minutes.

Use the 5-to-7-day line as the one you plan around. Sales are described as final, and sellers report slow refunds and thin support when a subscription was the mistake.

The Tip That Wants a Subscription

The complaint that repeats, from new sellers with no followers, is a $10 to $15 tip within minutes of the profile going live. Opening the message, seeing the sender or cashing the tip requires a subscription.

The agreement lists $9.99 as the 7-day Basic plan. After that payment, the buyer is often gone and the message with them.

That is not a welcome bonus. It is a reason to close the tab.

A real buyer does not need you to rent the shop for a week before they can say hello. The site also says both sides are ID-verified, which is hard to square with a tipper who appears instantly and then vanishes.

Watermarks and private galleries are real tools when a real buyer is on the other end. They do not rehabilitate the tip.

If you still list here, keep every charge written down, never send a file before the platform payment clears, and treat a day-one tip as a warning. Do not hand over bank details because a popup is impatient.

Support has a reputation for being hard to reach once the week is paid.

Who the Traffic Actually Is

The homepage talks about thousands of verified buyers, and marketing lines talk about a much larger creator count. Independent traffic notes from the same period showed a shop that buys a lot more visits than it earns from people searching for it.

Paid traffic can be real buyers. It can also be a crowd that makes a new profile look busy while the only urgent message is the tip.

Seller ID checks at onboarding are a plus. They are not a plus large enough to carry a 20 percent fee, a weekly plan and a review-site warning.

There is no app to make the per-item uploads less fiddly. Title, watermark and price every file yourself.

That granularity is control, and it is also a chore the collection-based shops do not make you repeat for each frame.

OnlyFeet FAQ

Is OnlyFeet legit?

It is a real operation. The footer names LORDLY in Paris, and sellers who reach a payout threshold do get paid.

Trustpilot had a breach-of-guidelines warning on the profile in June 2026 and had removed fake reviews. The site's own 4.5 is not a substitute.

How much does OnlyFeet charge sellers?

Basic is a platform fee of $9.99 for 7 days, $19.97 for 90 days or $39 lifetime, plus a 20 percent service fee on buyer payments. Pro is $14.99, $29.97 or $79, and the service fee drops to 0 percent.

The fee is charged even if you sell nothing.

Why did I get a tip immediately?

New sellers describe a $10 to $15 tip within minutes, which can be opened only after a subscription. The buyer often goes silent once the plan is paid.

How fast are OnlyFeet payouts?

The site has said daily, 5 to 7 business days, and, in a testimonial, 30 minutes. Plan for the business-week line.

Does OnlyFeet have an app?

No. Listing, messaging and uploads happen in the browser, one file at a time.

Should a new seller join OnlyFeet?

Not as a main shop. The weekly fee plus 20 percent is a hard way to learn the niche, and the instant tip is a bad introduction.

A seller who already has an audience can price Pro against a month of real sales. Everyone else should start elsewhere.
