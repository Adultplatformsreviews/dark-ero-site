Beginners and working creators ask different questions.

A first account needs a short setup, a realistic wait for the first payment, and a clear rule on who can see the posts. People who already earn care more about commission tiers and analytics.

The twelve names below are ordered by how soon a new seller is likely to see a first sale, not by a single review score. That is why the scores do not fall in rank order.

The order and the fee figures follow a public beginner ranking we rewrote in our own words. Where we already have a review, that page is linked.

A shorter companion list lives at our best creator platforms guide.

SpicyRanked discloses that FeetFinder is an affiliate partner on its beginner platform ranking. Our SpicyRanked review explains how that site scores platforms.

Adults only. Every platform here expects sellers to be 18 or older.

This page is general information, not a promise of income.

Twelve beginner platforms in 2026

1. FeetFinder: niche buyers without an outside audience

FeetFinder is a feet-only marketplace. The signup path walks through an ID check, content rules and pricing in one guided sequence.

The unusual part for a new seller is the buyer pool that already exists. Most subscription sites expect you to bring followers.

FeetFinder routes people who came for this niche toward new listings.

A published beginner guide puts typical first sales at 7 to 14 days, then a later range of about $200 to $500 a month for people who post steadily, with some established sellers clearing $3,000 a month. Those are reported ranges, not a Dark-Ero measurement, and sales are not guaranteed.

Seller IDs are checked before anyone can list. The terms say users may have to verify age and identity depending on where they live, and payments run through billing partners rather than person to person.

That setup is meant to cut one beginner risk: a chargeback weeks after a sale.

Basic starts at $4.99 a month or $14.99 a year, and the service fee on Basic is 15%, so the seller keeps 85%. Premium starts at $14.99 a month, with a 10% fee, so the seller keeps 90%.

Our review scores FeetFinder 8.0 out of 10. It is a poor fit if you want to sell outside feet content.

For the wider niche, see best sites to sell feet pics.

  • Setup: about 30 minutes, with guided onboarding.
  • First sale: often cited at 7 to 14 days when buyers are already on the site.
  • Fee: 15% on Basic, 10% on Premium.
  • Best for: beginners with no audience who want this niche.

2. OnlyFans: the name fans already know

OnlyFans is the platform most beginners have heard of. If you already have followers, that name makes a subscription easier to explain.

The same fame does not help a cold start. The site does little to show a new page to strangers, so earnings can sit near zero until outside posts send people in.

Setup takes longer than a niche shop because you make more product choices up front. A published guide puts that work at about 2 to 4 hours, plus a follow-up week if the first settings were guesses.

The platform fee is 20% on every tier, so creators keep 80%, and signup is free. Most methods cash out from $20.

Our review scores it 7.0 out of 10. The walkthrough how to start an OnlyFans account covers the decisions, and sites like OnlyFans covers the substitutes.

  • Setup: about 2 to 4 hours, including configuration.
  • First earnings: depend on the audience you already have.
  • Fee: 20%, with no cheaper rate at higher volume.
  • Best for: beginners who can send existing followers.

3. Fansly: similar subscriptions, steadier rules

Fansly opened in 2020 for creators who wanted a subscription site with more predictable adult-content rules than OnlyFans. That same point matters on day one.

The basics match a subscription shop: tiers, custom sales, pay-per-message and monthly renewals. The dashboard and multi-tier setup let a beginner test prices without rebuilding the page.

Discovery is a little stronger than on OnlyFans because the site still pushes newer creators in promotional rotations. Cold starts can move slightly faster.

The maths still favours people who bring their own audience.

Creators keep 80%, signup is free, and cash-out starts at $20. SpicyRanked scores Fansly 7.0 on this list.

Our own review uses a separate score, so read that page before you treat 7.0 as ours.

  • Setup: about 1 to 2 hours.
  • Fee: 20%, the same headline cut as OnlyFans.
  • Trade-off: a smaller audience and a name fewer casual fans recognise.
  • Best for: beginners who want tier flexibility and clearer adult rules.

4. Patreon: a non-adult path

Patreon is the only name on this list that does not ask you to frame the work as adult content. It is built for ongoing creative output, with tier templates you can adopt instead of designing from scratch.

Some artistic body content can sit inside a broader creative frame. Enforcement gets conservative when a post is an edge case, so this is a weak home for explicit sales.

Signup to a first published tier is often under an hour. There is little organic discovery, and subscribers expect a regular publishing rhythm.

People who post in bursts tend to lose them.

The platform fee is a flat 10%, plus payment processing, currency conversion and payout fees. Creator signup is free.

It fits a beginner who wants a mainstream membership, not a catalog of one-off adult clips.

  • Setup: under an hour with the built-in tier templates.
  • Fee: 10% platform fee, before processing and payout costs.
  • Limit: stricter content rules than dedicated adult sites.
  • Best for: creative work you can publish on a schedule.

5. ManyVids: sell a piece, skip the monthly promise

ManyVids has sold individual clips since 2014. Buyers expect to pay per piece, which removes the pressure to publish every month just to keep subscribers.

A strong clip can keep selling for weeks. New work can wait until it is ready.

The promotional tools are what give a new seller visibility that pure subscription sites usually do not.

The cut is higher because per-piece processing and those promos cost more to run. People who join the promos can make that up in volume.

A passive shop usually earns less than a subscription page would.

The standard tier takes 40%, and Premier takes 35%. Creator signup is free.

A published guide puts first earnings at about 2 to 6 weeks when the seller uses the promo tools.

  • Setup: about an hour.
  • Fee: 40% standard, 35% on Premier.
  • Best for: strong single pieces and an irregular posting calendar.
  • Weaker for: a long relationship with the same subscriber.

6. Loyalfans: premium prices, selective entry

Loyalfans rarely makes sense as a first platform unless a niche audience will already pay premium rates. It aims at older subscribers who spend several times what a mainstream fan spends.

That can mean higher monthly earnings from fewer people, if the niche is real. Onboarding is stricter.

Casual setups that pass on OnlyFans or Fansly can be rejected, so plan on a more polished page.

Creator payout is 80% to 85%, depending on tier, which beats the standard 80% on most subscription rivals. Signup is free and waits on approval.

Pay-per-message and customs are built in.

It is a slow ramp if you have no niche audience yet. Treat it as a second step, not the only account, until the content standard is one you can meet.

  • Setup: about 2 to 3 hours, plus an approval review.
  • Payout: 80% to 85% to the creator.
  • Best for: a defined niche and a production setup that looks finished.

7. AdmireMe.VIP: a UK privacy angle

AdmireMe.VIP is the pick when the seller is in the UK and data rules matter more than global scale. It operates under European GDPR limits on how creator and buyer data can be collected, kept and shared.

People who later leave have deletion routes that many US-based platforms do not offer. On-site privacy controls are more granular than the mainstream defaults.

The audience is concentrated in the UK, so UK and European creators tend to convert better here than on a US-default site. US creators usually see a weaker match.

Support is described as native English during UK working hours, which helps during a first setup. The platform fee is 25%, so the creator keeps 75%.

Signup is free. A published note puts first earnings for UK creators at about 2 to 6 weeks.

  • Setup: about 1 to 2 hours.
  • Fee: 25% platform fee.
  • Best for: UK beginners who want GDPR-style controls over a bigger US audience.

8. JustForFans: male and LGBTQ+ discovery

General subscription sites often surface women first and park other categories in a side tab. JustForFans treats male and LGBTQ+ creators as the main audience.

For those sellers, discovery works differently. Earnings per post can beat a broader site even when the total audience is smaller, because the buyers came looking for that category.

The smaller scale also means more creator-to-creator contact, including collaborations that are harder to find on a huge general site. Sellers outside those audiences get less from the same features.

The platform fee is 30%, so the creator keeps 70%. Signup is free.

A published guide puts first earnings at about 2 to 4 weeks when the audience match is real.

  • Setup: about 1 to 2 hours.
  • Fee: 30% platform fee.
  • Best for: male and LGBTQ+ beginners. A weaker default for women-led pages.

9. AVN Stars: industry names, steeper cold start

AVN Stars is run by the AVN Media Network, a long-running adult-industry organisation. That history decides who ramps quickly.

Performers who already have industry recognition can be found by buyers who know those credits. A cold start is shorter than on a general creator site for that group.

The buyers skew older and more likely to come back than a casual impulse subscriber. The smaller audience is the trade for that spend.

Without industry ties, the curve is steeper, because the audience expects affiliated names rather than a hobby page. The fee is 25%, so the creator keeps 75%.

Signup is free and pending review. Setup is described as about 2 hours plus a credential check.

  • First earnings: days if you already have industry recognition, weeks from a cold start.
  • Fee: 25% platform fee.
  • Best for: working performers crossing into a creator page.

10. Passes: mainstream, and not an adult site

Passes launched in 2022, founded by Lucy Guo, as a mainstream creator app that left adult content out from the start. The audience behaves more like social-platform fans than adult-clip buyers.

Tools such as direct messages, livestreams and engagement features follow patterns people already know from Instagram or TikTok. Setup for someone coming from those apps is often under an hour.

The platform fee is 10%, so the creator keeps 90%. Signup is free.

The difference from Patreon is positioning and audience, not a dramatic fee gap.

Explicit work does not belong here. The track record is also shorter than Patreon's, and the name is less familiar.

  • Setup: under an hour.
  • Fee: 10% platform fee.
  • Best for: non-adult creators who already have a social following.

11. Unfiltrd: a newer adult subscription cut

Unfiltrd is a newer adult subscription site built around complaints about high cuts, slow payouts and shifting rules. Creators keep 85%, which is a 15% platform fee.

That extra share matters more in a thin first month than it does once income is stable. Because the site is still growing, newer creators show up in promotional rotations more often than on the largest sites.

The cold start is shorter than on OnlyFans, and the ceiling is lower because the audience is smaller. Payment edge cases and policy consistency are less proven.

Signup is free, and setup is described as 30 to 60 minutes. A published note puts typical first earnings at about 2 to 4 weeks.

Use it beside a more established account, not as the only income.

  • Fee: 15% platform fee, 85% to the creator.
  • Best for: beginners who want a lower cut during the ramp, with a backup platform.

12. MYM Fans: French-language subscriptions

MYM Fans started in France in 2017 and became the main French-language creator subscription site. Strength sits in France, Belgium, French-speaking Switzerland, Quebec and parts of French-speaking Africa.

French-speaking sellers get native-language support, France-specific tax forms and banking that fits French and wider EU payouts better than many US platforms.

French-speaking buyers look for French content, so conversion is often better here than on an English-default site with a translated caption. Sellers who do not work in French rarely get that benefit.

The platform fee is 25%, so the creator keeps 75%. Signup is free.

Setup is about 1 to 2 hours, and a published guide puts first earnings for French creators at about 2 to 4 weeks.

  • Fee: 25% platform fee.
  • Best for: beginners whose audience is francophone.

How to pick the first platform

Three questions decide the start.

Do you already have followers? If yes, OnlyFans, Fansly, Patreon or Passes convert that audience with less explanation.

If no, FeetFinder's built-in buyers, or a specialty site that matches who you are, is the usual first stop.

What are you selling? Adult subscriptions cluster on OnlyFans, Fansly, Loyalfans, JustForFans, Unfiltrd and AVN Stars.

Non-adult work fits Patreon and Passes. Feet content fits FeetFinder, and single clips fit ManyVids.

Where is the audience? UK sellers get a privacy case for AdmireMe.VIP.

French-speaking sellers get MYM Fans. Male and LGBTQ+ sellers get JustForFans.

Industry-connected performers get AVN Stars.

Most people who last do not stay on one site forever. The pattern that holds up is one fitting platform first, then a second account after the first one has traction.

Fanvue is another subscription site in the OnlyFans mould. A published note says creators keep 85% in the first month and 80% after that.

Our Fanvue review records the intro as a 15% fee for the first 30 days after the ID check, then 20%.

Apps, safety and newer names

Most adult creator platforms do not have an app in the Apple or Google stores, because those stores reject this kind of content. The practical substitute is the mobile site, sometimes saved to the home screen.

Patreon and Passes, which are not adult platforms, do have store apps. Treat any third-party download that claims to be an official adult app as unsafe.

Major platforms use ordinary encryption and tokenised card processing. The beginner risks that actually show up are behavioural: off-platform payment, a weak password, and chargebacks.

Our Is OnlyFans safe guide covers the verification pattern those sites share.

Newer 2026 names split three ways. Mainstream non-adult apps such as Passes keep raising money.

Adult subscription alternatives compete on the cut. Niche shops keep appearing for one audience.

A new platform is a better second income than a only income, because the operations are less proven.

Where a beginner should start

There is no single best platform. FeetFinder is the shortest path in this ranking when you have no audience and the work is feet content.

OnlyFans, Fansly, Patreon and Passes fit people who can already send followers. Specialty sites match a region, a language or a demographic that the big general sites bury.

Income still comes from posting and from an audience, not from the platform discovering you by itself. Pick the shop that matches the situation you actually have.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best creator platforms for beginners in 2026?

It depends on the start you have. FeetFinder is the fastest path in this ranking if you have no audience and you sell feet content.

OnlyFans converts followers you already have. Patreon and Passes are the non-adult options.

No single site wins for every beginner.

How do you become a content creator from scratch?

Pick one platform that matches your situation, spend the first 30 to 60 days posting and finding an audience, then add a second platform after the first one shows traction. Starting on several sites at once usually means none of them move.

How long until a beginner earns?

On FeetFinder, a published guide puts first sales at 7 to 14 days because buyers are already on the site. On OnlyFans, Fansly and Patreon, the wait is the time it takes your own audience to show up, which can be days or months.

ManyVids is often cited at 2 to 6 weeks if you use the promo tools.

Can you start without paying a setup fee?

Yes on Patreon, OnlyFans, Fansly, ManyVids, JustForFans, AdmireMe.VIP, Passes, Unfiltrd and MYM Fans. Those accounts are free, and the platform takes a cut of earnings.

FeetFinder charges a seller plan, from $4.99 a month or $14.99 a year on Basic, plus 15% on Basic or 10% on Premium. Regular sales can make that cheaper than a free site that takes 20%.

Few sales make the free sites cheaper.

Which of these have mobile apps?

Most adult platforms are not in the Apple or Google stores. Use the mobile website, or a home-screen web app where the site offers one.

Patreon and Passes, which are not adult platforms, have ordinary store apps.

How does Fanvue compare for a beginner?

Fanvue is a subscription site in the same mould as OnlyFans. A published note says creators keep 85% in the first month and 80% after that.

It still depends on an outside audience. Our Fanvue review covers the 15% intro fee and the later 20% rate.

What if you do not want to go viral?

Most working creators do not earn from a viral post. Steady posting, more than one platform, and a specific niche build a smaller group of buyers who come back.

That pattern is more stable than chasing a mass audience.

Are these platforms safe for a first account?

The major sites use standard encryption and tokenised payments. Beginner losses usually come from behaviour, not from the platform leaking a card.

ID checks and on-site billing, as on FeetFinder, reduce chargeback risk. Read the safety notes on our OnlyFans review before you upload an ID.
