What Is Patreon?

Patreon is a membership service started in 2013 by musician Jack Conte and developer Sam Yam. Fans support a creator with a monthly or yearly membership, or they buy single posts, collections and digital downloads from a built-in shop.

The company behind it is Patreon, Inc., headquartered on Townsend Street in San Francisco. It is a long-running, venture-backed business with public, dated policies, which is more than many adult creator sites can say.

Most of its creators run podcasts, YouTube channels, comics, games or music projects. Adult creators are welcome only inside a narrow lane, and that lane is much stricter than on Fansly or OnlyFans.

How Patreon Works for Creators

You set up a page, create membership tiers and decide which posts are free, open to every paying member or locked to a higher tier. Free members can follow you and upgrade later, which turns the page into a simple sales funnel.

The toolkit is broad. It covers monthly and annual billing, one-time purchases, native video, livestreams, email newsletters, group chats, DMs, member analytics, free trials and discount codes.

Subscription Billing Changes

Members are charged on the day they join and then on the same date each cycle. If a card fails, Patreon keeps retrying for around 30 days before the person drops to a free membership.

Creators who still bill per post or on the first of the month must switch to subscription billing by 1 November 2026. Patreon says it will move late pages over automatically and pause billing until new prices are confirmed, so check your settings now.

The iOS App Catch

Patreon has official iOS and Android apps. When a fan pays inside the iOS app, Apple takes 30%, and Patreon raises the price for that buyer by default.

US fans can still pay on the mobile website to avoid that markup.

Patreon Fees and Payouts

Fans join for free and creators launch a page for free. Patreon earns by keeping a share of what you sell, and the 2026 headline is simple: a flat 10% for any page published after 4 August 2025.

  • Platform fee: 10% on new pages. Older pages keep legacy rates between 5% and 12% only while they stay published.
  • Card processing (US): 2.9% plus $0.30 on payments above $3, including Apple Pay, PayPal and Venmo.
  • PayPal or Venmo outside the US: 3.9% plus $0.30.
  • Currency conversion: 2.5% when a fan pays in a different currency from your payout currency.
  • US direct deposit: $0.25 per payout.
  • PayPal payout: 1%, minimum $0.25, capped at $20, with a $10 minimum withdrawal.
  • Payoneer: $1 per payout for non-US creators, $25 minimum.

Put together, a $10 web sale in US dollars leaves you about $8.41 before payout fees. That is noticeably more than the 80% you would keep on most adult subscription sites, but only if your content stays inside Patreon's rules.

One trap is worth repeating. If a legacy page is ever unpublished, it comes back on the 10% plan, so do not take an old page offline casually.

Patreon Adult Content Rules

This is the section adult creators should read twice. Patreon allows sexual, sensual and suggestive work, but only to paying members who have confirmed they are over 18, and only on pages flagged as Adult/18+.

The hard line is real sexual activity. Photos or video of real or hyperrealistic adults engaged in sex are not permitted.

Illustrated, animated, written and audio adult work with fictional characters is allowed if it follows the other rules.

Anything public must stay clean. That includes your avatar, banner, tier descriptions, free posts and shop previews.

The Commerce Guidelines also forbid selling porn or access to porn sites.

Age and Consent Checks

Payment partners such as Visa and Mastercard require Adult/18+ creators to verify their age. Anyone else who appears in your work must also be verified and sign written consent, and you must keep those records ready if Patreon asks.

Deepfakes and other synthetic sexual images of real people are banned, as is any sexualised portrayal of adults as minors.

Is Patreon Safe and Private?

For creators and fans alike, Patreon is a legitimate and stable company. The real risk for adult creators is not fraud; it is losing a page because something slipped past the content rules.

Billing and Refunds

Statements show lines such as PATREON* Membership or PAYPAL *PATREON IN. The Patreon name is visible, but not the creator you support, which is fairly discreet for fans.

Refunds are not standard. Patreon says it may make exceptions at its discretion, and App Store purchases may need to go through Apple.

Privacy and Data

The Privacy Policy, last updated in May 2026, says Patreon does not sell personal data. It does use data for recommendations and personalised ads, which you can limit in marketing settings.

When a fan joins, the creator sees the full profile name and email address. Fans who value privacy should check both before paying.

Creators also grant Patreon a broad, perpetual licence to use posted work to run the service, while keeping ownership.

Patreon Pros and Cons

What Works

  • A single 10% fee for new pages, roughly half the cut on most adult fan sites.
  • Fully published tables for processing, currency and payout costs.
  • Monthly and annual billing, shop items, video, chats and newsletters in one place.
  • Official apps on iOS and Android.
  • Billing lines that hide which creator a fan supports.

What Does Not

  • No real sexual activity, which rules out a lot of adult photo and video work.
  • Public areas must be fully clean, which limits marketing on the page itself.
  • Apple's 30% cut on in-app iOS purchases.
  • Refunds only at Patreon's discretion.
  • Very little built-in discovery, so you still need outside traffic.

Patreon FAQ

Does Patreon allow NSFW content in 2026?

Yes, on pages marked Adult/18+ and only behind a paywall. Fictional adult art, writing and audio are allowed, but sexual activity between real people is banned.

How much does Patreon take from creators?

New pages pay a 10% platform fee plus payment processing, which is 2.9% plus $0.30 for most US payments over $3. Payout fees are small extras.

Can I sell feet pics on Patreon?

Tasteful, non-explicit foot photography can sit on a paywalled 18+ page. Explicit fetish content with real people risks removal, so a feet marketplace is safer for that.

What shows on a fan's bank statement?

A line such as PATREON* Membership. The creator's name is not shown.

Does Patreon give refunds?

Not as a rule. Patreon may refund at its discretion, and iOS in-app purchases are handled by Apple.

Is Patreon better than OnlyFans?

On fees, yes, at 10% against 20%. On adult content freedom and audience, OnlyFans is far ahead, so many creators use both.
