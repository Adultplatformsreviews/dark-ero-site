What Is BornToBeFuck?

BornToBeFuck is a free adult video site. Clips are listed by category and by channel, the site says new videos arrive daily, and a VIP offer is meant to remove the adverts.

The terms name Novamart Limited of Hong Kong as the publisher.

The homepage also links to a live-cam section, a dating section, a shop and a Telegram channel. Those are side doors.

The product you land on is a grid of explicit clips, and it is for adults only.

The legal notice names the company, number 76903480, an address in Kwun Tong, and Vladislavs Ilcenko as director of publication. We could not match those details to a public registry, so they remain the site's own statements.

Response headers match Cloudflare, which the notice names as host and storage. VAT registration is described as still in progress.

Four role addresses are printed, for contact, legal, abuse and data protection.

The legal notice and the terms are dated 11 June 2026 and written in French on the French edition. Video files also load from a separate streaming domain.

We could not find who operates that domain.

How the Site Works

You get a grid, a search bar, categories, tags and channels. The site's own banner says a free video takes four ad opens.

It also warns that a VPN or an ad blocker stops playback.

The video page we opened did not play in the page. The player area said the clip lived only on Telegram and offered a button that leaves the site.

We could not tell how many pages work that way.

Two money features sit beside the player. B-Bucks are an in-house credit the wallet text describes for top-ups, shop purchases, donations, live tips and earnings from views.

VIP is the paid tier the terms say removes adverts.

A homepage counter showed 6,543,849 visitors this month, and the same figure sat on the English and French pages minutes apart. We could not verify it.

Treat it as a marketing line.

The terms ask uploaders to confirm they hold the rights and the consent of everyone in a clip. The DMCA page says the site does not host videos on its servers.

The legal notice describes a video hosting service with storage at Cloudflare. Both pages are live on the same site.

The homepage copy describes the collection as free photos and clips of models and streamers from subscription platforms, and the keyword tags include the word leaks. We did not inspect a single file, so this review makes no claim that any given clip is licensed.

Creators who find their work there have a removal path, covered below.

The general steps, when a clip of your work turns up on a tube site, are in our guide on how to support creators directly, and why leak sites cost more than they seem. Paying a stranger who offers a private takedown in chat is a second problem.

What the €1 VIP Actually Says

Watching is free if you sit through the ads. The VIP window shows a banner for €1, a countdown, a 30-day plan, and payment by bank card, USDT or Bitcoin.

We did not pay, so we cannot say what is billed, when, or by whom. The same window says you will be sent to a VIP Telegram support channel.

A separate VIP landing page tells a different story: a $1 trial, lifetime access, more than 1,200 videos, cancel in one click, and a bank descriptor along the lines of SSL MELAS. Article 8 of the terms describes a monthly subscription that renews on its own, with no trial period, whose job is to remove adverts.

Article 10 says prices are in euros including tax and that a third party takes the payment.

A euro price, a dollar price, a trial, a monthly renewal and lifetime access cannot all be the offer. The terms do not state a renewal price.

B-Bucks pack prices show up as placeholders until you log in. Screenshot the checkout before you confirm it.

Per the terms, you cancel from the account or by email to the contact address. Cancellation takes effect at the end of the paid month, with no pro-rata refund.

Starting the service at once is also written as waiving the 14-day withdrawal right. The VIP window mentions a money-back guarantee.

We found no matching refund rule in the terms. Keep the receipt.

The Age Gate and the Arcom Notice

The English homepage asks you to confirm you are at least 21, with a button that says you are 21 or older. The French edition uses the same pattern for 18.

Article 5.1 of the terms reserves the service for adults of 18 and over. The visible gate, the French gate and the contract use three different lines, and the site does not explain the gap.

Article 5.3 and the privacy policy both say the publisher applies an age check that follows the Arcom framework. Arcom's own record describes a simple declaration.

Arcom issued formal notice 2026-459 on 22 September 2026. An officer recorded the access method on 21 July 2026.

A letter of observations went out on 28 July. The authority announced the action on 29 July.

A second record on 21 September found the same entry method. The decision on 22 September says Novamart Limited sent no observations.

The publisher had 15 days from notification to stop minors reaching the content.

The notice is about the gate. It is not a ruling on the videos, and it is not itself a block.

As of 29 October 2026 we found no public report that the site had been blocked in France, and the gate we saw was still a declaration button. We could not see what a visitor in France is shown.

Ad steps are the other practical risk. The page code includes an ad-network verification tag, and tube sites with this many forced opens are a common source of pop-ups.

We did not run a malware scan, so this is a caution from the pattern. Our guide on how to tell a real adult site from a malware trap covers the prompts worth closing, including anything that asks you to install software in order to press play.

Use a separate browser profile if you open the site at all, and keep the system updated. A download offered by a pop-up is the window to dismiss.

The site publishes a DMCA page, a removal page and a 2257 page. DMCA notices are requested in English.

Removal is a single email. The 2257 page says its own compliance checks may miss things.

The legal notice also names an abuse address. A French-edition notice channel under the EU Digital Services Act returned a not-found page when it was opened, so the email is the channel that exists.

Creators who need the paperwork for a takedown can follow the ranking-site guide to OnlyFans leaks, scams and removal, then use the emails this site prints. Keep copies of the links and of every message you send.

Privacy and Tracking

The privacy policy is in French and dated 11 June 2026. It lists account data such as name, email, username and password, technical data such as IP address and logs, and payment references.

Card details are handled by a processor whose name appears at checkout, and the site says it does not store the card itself. Uploaders may be asked for identity documents.

GDPR rights are cited, a data-protection email is named, and complaints can go to the French regulator CNIL. Data may be stored in Hong Kong and the United States.

The legal notice and the privacy policy say analytics run only after consent, through a banner on the first visit. The pages we read contained a Google Tag Manager container, and we could not see a consent banner in the page source.

Whether a banner appears in an ordinary browser is unverified.
