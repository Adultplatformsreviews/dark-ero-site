Browser hygiene for private adult browsing

A handful of settings does more for your privacy than any single tool you can buy.

Privacy for adult browsing is mostly not about exotic tools. It is about a few browser settings that most people never change, each of which closes a common leak.

None of this costs anything.

Use a separate browser profile

The simplest, highest-impact step is a dedicated browser profile — or a separate browser entirely — for adult browsing. It keeps history, cookies, logins and autofill walled off from your everyday browsing, and it means a shared or borrowed device never surprises you.

Understand what private mode does and doesn't

Incognito or private mode stops your browser from saving history and cookies locally. It does not hide your activity from your internet provider, your network, or the sites you visit.

It is a local-cleanup tool, not an anonymity tool — useful, but only for what it actually does.

Private mode protects you from the next person who uses your device. A VPN protects you from the network.

They solve different problems; using one doesn't cover the other.

Turn off autofill for the profile

Autofill is convenient and it leaks. On an adult-browsing profile, turn off form and payment autofill so a saved address or card can't surface where you don't want it.

Two minutes in settings, permanently useful.

Add a blocker, keep it updated

A reputable ad and script blocker cuts tracking and neutralises most malicious ads before they load. Combined with a separate profile and an up-to-date browser, it covers the large majority of real-world privacy risk in adult browsing — no subscription required.

The stack, in order

Separate profile, blocker, updated browser, and a VPN when you are on a network you don't control. In that order.

Each layer covers a different gap, and together they are more than enough for almost everyone.
