Three numbers that are not the same

A mean adds every payout and divides by every account, including the ones that never post. A median is the middle account when you line them up from lowest to highest, and a dashboard is one person's gross.

When a small share of accounts earns most of the money, the mean sits above what a typical creator makes. Swapping the three numbers is how this topic goes wrong.

Gross is what fans paid. Creator payout is 80 percent of that after the platform's cut, and take-home is the payout minus tax, tools, promotion and any manager.

What the 2025 accounts say

Fenix filed its fiscal 2025 report, the year ended 30 November 2025, at Companies House in 2026. Fan payments were $7.8 billion, up from $7.2 billion, and about $6.2 billion was paid to creators.

Creator accounts were 5.063 million, up from 4.634 million, and fan accounts were 437.1 million. The report describes the split as $4 to creators for every $1 the group keeps, which is the familiar 80/20.

Divide $6.2 billion by 5.063 million accounts and you get about $1,225 a year, or about $102 a month. Inactive and abandoned accounts sit in that denominator, so the mean is not what an active poster makes.

Variety and the BBC reported about 2.5 million active creator accounts in that year, a press figure rather than a line we could read in the filing. Divide the same $6.2 billion by 2.5 million and you get about $207 a month, which is still a derived average lifted by the biggest earners, not a median.

OnlyFans has not published 2026 results yet. The fiscal 2026 year ends on 30 November 2026, and those accounts are not the ones in front of you today.

In May 2026 Fenix sold a 16 percent stake to Architect Capital for $535 million, a deal reported at a company value around $3.15 billion. That is a price for the company, not a wage for a creator.

Where the $180 came from

On 24 April 2020 Thomas Hollands published a sample of about 1,000 accounts. He estimated fans from likes, used the lowest listed subscription price, left tips out, and said the biggest accounts were not in the sample.

After a 20 percent cut he put median take-home near $136, and the gross median that people still quote is about $180 a month. Most accounts in the sample took home under $145, and the most common values were $0 and then $4.99.

The top 1 percent of that sample took about a third of the money. Stretching that old share onto the fiscal 2025 payout is a thought experiment, about $40,000 a year per top-percentile account if the shape still held, and it is not a measured 2026 salary.

A claim that the top 1 percent earn $20,000 to $100,000 a month does not fit that share of the filing. It would require the top slice to take far more of the pool than the 2020 sample showed.

The public ceiling is a different object, and it is collected in our OnlyFans top earners page. Sophie Rain's January 2026 recording was read as about $101.2 million all-time gross, with a later self-shown total near $110 million in May.

A subscription price is not an income

A study of creator pages in October 2026 found a median paid subscription around $12.99. At that price the creator keeps about $10.39 per subscriber after the 20 percent fee, which is a price, not a count of subscribers.

One subscriber at $12.99 does not make a month. You would need a crowd of them, plus messages and tips, before the filing's per-account mean even comes into view.

Verdict

Count every account and fiscal 2025 looks like about $102 a month after the fee. Count a 2020 sample and the middle looks like about $180 gross, with a pile of accounts at zero.

Neither number is a salary OnlyFans promised you. Adults only, and the dashboard screenshots are the ceiling, not the job.

FAQ

How much do OnlyFans creators make a month?

The fiscal 2025 filing works out to about $102 a month if you divide creator payouts by every creator account, including inactive ones. A press count of active accounts lifts that derived mean to about $207, which is still not a median.

Is the average $180 a month?

That figure is Thomas Hollands' April 2020 sample median, about $180 gross, from roughly 1,000 accounts with tips excluded. It is not an official 2026 average.

How much do creators keep?

Creators keep 80 percent of fan payments, and OnlyFans keeps 20 percent. Take-home is whatever remains after tax, tools, promotion and any manager.

What is the top of the range?

Self-shown dashboards reach about $101 million to $110 million in lifetime gross for one creator, which is not audited and not typical. OnlyFans has said 5,076 creators earned more than $1 million since 2016.

Does a $13 subscription equal $13 of income?

No, the creator keeps about 80 percent of the price, and one subscriber is not a month of earnings. A median price is not a median income.

Have 2026 earnings been published?

Not as a full year, because fiscal 2026 ends on 30 November 2026. The latest audited creator-pool figures are the fiscal 2025 accounts.
