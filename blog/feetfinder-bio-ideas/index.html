A formula that fits the box

FeetFinder's FAQ says other users see your username, profile photos, bio, ethnicity and foot size. Address and photo ID stay private for verification and payouts, so you do not need to repeat the size in the bio.

Name the thing you shoot in the words buyers type, then say whether the gallery holds photo sets, short clips, or customs. Add one limit, such as feet only or customs closed this week, and invite a message on the site.

The seller agreement says the profile must not display phone numbers, street addresses, last names, email addresses or URLs. The same ban covers the username and the photos.

Lines you can rewrite

These are writing examples, not real profiles and not proven sales copy. If the gallery does not contain the thing the line promises, do not publish the line.

"Daylight pedicures, close and unfiltered, with custom shades open by message on this site." Swap the polish detail for whatever you actually reshoot each week.

"Soft soles on a plain floor, in sets and short clips, with custom angles only after I confirm them here." That version tells a sole buyer what the folder holds.

"High arches from the side, new sets when they are ready, customs reviewed before I shoot." Arch buyers search the word, so put it in the first clause.

"Striped socks and knee highs I own, with the colours named on each set." Mention worn items only if you really pack and post them yourself.

What stays out

Skip your legal name, city, school, gym, workplace and any landmark a neighbour would recognise. A distinctive tattoo can identify you with the face nowhere in frame, so cover it or leave it out if privacy is the point.

Face is optional. FeetFinder says sellers who show a full body and face often have a better chance of sales, and that trade is yours, not a requirement.

The profile photo has to reflect you, which means your own foot or your own face, never a stock image or someone else's set. Skip promises of instant replies and free samples, because the second one is a common way to collect files for nothing.

Usernames

Buyers can search sellers by username, so a short name they can retype is worth more than a clever string of numbers. Try a niche word plus a plain noun, then test five options and keep the one the signup form accepts.

Leave out your real name, birth year, hometown and old social handles. The terms also ban usernames that impersonate FeetFinder, its staff, or another user, and the company can disable a name when an account breaks the rules.

FAQ

What should a FeetFinder bio say?

Name the niche, what buyers can get, one boundary, and a next step on the site. Two or three sentences are enough.

Should I put my city or my real name in the bio?

Leave both out, along with your phone, email, social handles and any link. The seller agreement bars those on the profile.

Do I need to list my foot size?

Buyers can already see foot size on the profile, so use the bio for the content instead. Repeat a size only if a set truly depends on it.

Can I show my face?

Yes, and FeetFinder says a full body and face can help sales, while feet-only profiles are allowed. The profile photo still has to be yours.

What makes a good username?

Short, easy to retype, and disconnected from your personal accounts. Avoid your real name, your birth year, and anything that impersonates the company or another user.

Can the bio mention PayPal or another app?

No, keep payment and chat on FeetFinder. Outside payment offers are both against the terms and a common scam step.
