What the click is built to do

A thumbnail and a fake play button ask you to install a codec, update Flash, allow notifications, or download a program. That is a malware pattern, and Flash has been dead for years.

Other pages ask for a Google or Apple login to prove your age, or a card for a free trial. The login is the theft, and the trial is the card.

Some results sit on a familiar .edu or .gov address, because insecure school and government sites get hijacked and filled with this spam. The domain looks trusted, and the page is not the institution.

Leave if the title pairs a creator's name with leaked, full dump, or free VIP, on a domain that is not their known site. A creator's real free wall is on their actual profile, and it is labelled as free.

If you are the person searching

Do not download the file, do not type the password, and do not pay the fake archive. The practical risk is your accounts, even when nobody is interested in prosecuting you for the visit.

Redistributing someone else's intimate posts can also be a civil or criminal problem, depending on where you are. The clean version of the same content is a subscription the creator actually sells.

If the posts are yours

Save the full URL, the time, and a screenshot that includes the address bar. You will need that bundle for every notice you send, and the pages move.

Report the copy to the host through whatever copyright or abuse form they publish, and report impersonation accounts on the social network where they appear. A fan who was tricked in a fake chat should be pointed at that report too.

OnlyFans runs a free stolen-content report, which asks for your name and a way to reach you. That form is the company's path, and it is not a paid service with a stranger's logo on it.

In the United States, a DMCA notice to the host or the search engine is the copyright route when you own the pictures or you are allowed to act for the owner. A proper notice identifies the work, the URL, and includes a statement under penalty of perjury, which is why a template from a random forum is a bad place to start.

Separately, the Take It Down Act tells covered platforms to remove nonconsensual intimate images, and known identical copies, within 48 hours of a valid request. The FTC's enforcement date for that duty is 19 May 2026, and the duty still does not reach every forum on earth.

If someone threatens to post your images unless you pay or send more, do not pay and do not send the file. Save the screenshots, block them, and report it to the platform and, in the US, to local police or the FBI.

Expect the copies to reappear. The realistic goal is fewer results and less traffic, not a single deletion that covers the whole internet.

FAQ

Are OnlyFans leak sites safe to open?

No. The usual click path is malware, a phishing login, or a card form.

Leave, and do not install the player they offer.

Can I get a refund from a leak site?

Assume no. The payment was the scam, and the content was never theirs to sell.

Tell your card issuer if the charge was unauthorised, and keep the receipt.

How does a creator request removal?

Save the URL and a screenshot, use OnlyFans' free stolen-content report, and send a copyright or intimate-image notice to the host. Keep a copy of everything you send.

Will one notice clear the internet?

No. Copies come back, and some hosts ignore mail.

The practical aim is fewer visible copies, one URL at a time.

Should I pay someone to delete leaks?

Do not pay a stranger who promises a total wipe, especially in gift cards or crypto. A real notice names the URLs first.
