Omegle launched on 25 March 2009 as Leif K-Brooks's one-button chat with a stranger. There was no profile and no age check, which is why it spread, and why it became impossible to keep safe.

By December 2022 the site was drawing on the order of 70 million visits a month. K-Brooks closed it on 8 November 2023, after lawsuits, safety criticism and new online-safety laws, and said the cost of fighting abuse had become more than one person could carry.

How this list is split

The first 15 are the no-account chats, ordered from the oldest roulette brands toward the newer clones. The next three are strong random-video apps that ask for a Google login or a check, so they do not belong in a no-sign-in promise.

The last three are adult cam-to-cam roulette for people 18 or older. They match you with another user, which is a different product from a performer cam site in our live cams category.

Chatroulette

Chatroulette is the 2009 original, and it is still the named roulette in 2026. You allow the camera and press start, with no registration, and the next button skips to another face.

It now says it uses AI and human moderation and an 18+ rule, and it offers a country filter. A credit called Quids pays for some extras, while the core random chat stays free, on a smaller crowd than its peak.

Camsurf

Camsurf is the clean no-account option: open the site, allow the camera, and you are in a chat in a few seconds. Nothing is meant to remain when you close the tab.

Country and language filters are part of the product, and it runs on the web plus iOS and Android apps, with users spread across a very wide set of countries. Free gender filtering is limited, and the pool is thinner than OmeTV at quiet hours.

Shagle

Shagle adds a country selector covering more than 70 countries, plus virtual masks if you do not want your face on camera. The core random video chat is free in the browser.

Gender filtering sits on a premium plan, and the free tier carries ads. Translation and virtual gifts are the extras once a chat is already going.

Chatspin

Chatspin pairs random video with AR face masks, which double as a way to hide your face. Gender, country and language filters are offered, along with automatic translation.

Core chat is free, gender filtering is premium, and there is a mobile app beside the website. Ads show on the free version.

Chatrandom

Chatrandom is one-click matching that has been around long enough to keep a crowd. Gender and location filters sit on the paid tier, and group rooms sit beside the one-to-one chat.

It runs in the browser and in an app. The free tier shows ads, which is the trade for the one-click start.

ChatHub

ChatHub is built around automatic translation, so a shared language is not required. It also offers interest matching, and registration is optional for basic video chat.

The pool is smaller than the biggest names. Some advanced features ask for an account after you have already started.

Bazoocam

Bazoocam adds mini-games you can play with the stranger you just met, which is a useful icebreaker. A moderation team on the order of 40 people is part of how the site describes itself, and it enforces an 18+ rule.

Gender filtering is available, and the interface stays simple. The crowd is smaller than the top names, and there is less beyond the core chat and the games.

OpenTalk

OpenTalk is browser video and text with no signup and no app install. It describes AI moderation, a one-tap report button and automatic bans.

An optional mobile app adds verified profiles for people who want a safer one-to-one connection later. The pool is newer, so it is smaller than the legacy roulettes.

Joingy

Joingy lets you pick text or video, add interest tags, and match without an account. It runs in desktop and mobile browsers and does not offer an official app.

Moderation leans on user reports. Use the next button early if a chat turns hostile, because the lighter review is the trade for the fast start.

Thundr

Thundr describes itself as a high-volume random chat, with a claim of over a million users a day, and it connects video or text without a signup. It runs on the web and in a mobile app.

Basic reporting is there, and the safety tooling is still younger than the older brands. Treat the million-a-day line as the site's own figure.

Chitchat.gg

Chitchat.gg is a calmer take on the same formula: free random text and video, no signup, and AI moderation on a minimal page. Matching is meant to be immediate.

The pool is smaller than OmeTV or Chatroulette, and the advanced filters are thin. That sparseness is the point if the old roulette felt chaotic.

Uhmegle

Uhmegle stays close to the original Omegle click: start, allow the camera, meet a stranger, with video and text and no signup. It describes reach across 190 or more countries, plus interest matching and one-tap reporting, all in the browser.

Moderation is lighter than the top of this list. Connections are often short, so the skip button does most of the work.

StrangerLine

StrangerLine covers anonymous text, voice and video in one no-signup tool, and it says the matches are people rather than bots. You can send a friend request before you disconnect if a conversation is worth keeping.

The core random chat is free, in the browser and on mobile. The pool is still smaller than the legacy sites.

OmeFree

OmeFree is a lightweight video chat with no signup, no download and no payment. It says the video is peer-to-peer WebRTC, so the stream goes between browsers, and that it collects no personal data.

Video and text are both there, and the site describes itself as ad-free. Filters are few, and the crowd is newer.

OmeTV

OmeTV is the all-round replacement most people mean: browser and mobile apps, country and gender selection, built-in translation, and no time limit or ad break in the way the site describes the free chat. Matches come quickly because the pool is one of the large ones.

Some devices or features still ask you to sign in or verify, so the no-account promise is not absolute on every screen. Bans can arrive quickly, and some regions skew heavily male.

Three that ask you to sign in

These three are random video chats people rank beside Omegle, and each one asks for a login or a check before the first match. They are listed apart so the first 15 stay no-account.

Emerald Chat

Emerald Chat markets interest tags that pair you with people who share them, plus text, one-to-one video and group rooms. You start with a Google account or a captcha, and a karma score tracks behavior under 24/7 AI and human review.

Gender filtering is on the paid tier. The login is the anti-bot step, and it is also why this is not a no-trace start.

Camdiv

Camdiv launched in 2025 and connects video, voice or text across a claimed 195 or more countries. A Google login and a one-time 18+ check take a few seconds, after which the people you meet still see you as anonymous.

The site describes AI face detection on frames to pull minors and explicit content. It is free of a paywall in the current write-up, and the pool is still growing.

Azar

Azar is the large random-video app, with a public claim of over 500 million downloads and users in 190 or more countries. It is owned by Match Group, pairs one-to-one video, and includes real-time voice translation.

The matching is more app-like than a browser roulette, and some features use a coin or reward balance. Moderation is part of the product, which is also why it feels less like a blank next button.

A short verdict

Open OmeTV or Chatroulette if you want the familiar roulette with a large or a famous pool. Open Camsurf or OpenTalk if the signup screen is the thing you refuse to touch, and open ChatHub if translation is the feature you will actually use.
