What Feetify Says It Is

Feetify is a website for buying and selling feet pictures and videos. The homepage title we saw was "Where to Sell and Buy Feet Pictures".

Official pages describe a site in the browser, not a phone-store app.

Joining is free. Seller pages say you can post some pictures and videos, and appear among active members, without paying.

Premium is the tier that unlocks messages, contacting buyers, custom sales, cash gifts, featured placement and the "100 percent" line.

That door matters. The FAQ says the majority of connections and sales are done through messaging, and that messaging is Premium.

A free account can hold photos. It is not the selling setup the site itself talks up.

The Terms of Service URL loads, and the body is the same promotional copy used elsewhere.

On 17 November 2026 that page claimed "500,000+ real active members" and did not name a company, a governing law or a contract.

We treat the member line as marketing. The page does not define what "active" counts.

The Sellers Paid page says it shows some of "7,000+ Premium Feetify models" who have been paid.

The same page's pager, the same day, said "Viewing 1 - 20 of 1,425 members" and ran to page 72.

Both figures are on the site. The gap is not proof of fraud, and 7,000 is not a total we confirmed by counting profiles.

The domain record lists a registration on 8 August 2019, registrar Namecheap, and a redacted registrant.

That date is not a launch announcement. The crypto page dates the $49 offer "From 9th Nov. 2021."

A price date is not a founding date.

The footer we saw was "© 2026 - Feetify.com". No address and no jurisdiction sat under it.

Similar names are easy to mix up. feetify.co is a separate guide, not this marketplace, and its fee language is not an official Feetify rate.

Unrelated image apps have also used the Feetify name. None of them open a seller account on feetify.com.

The $49 Cryptocurrency Plan

On 17 November 2026 the crypto upgrade page was readable without an account. Premium was $49 for 12 months, paid in cryptocurrency.

The page says you can pay with Cash App, Coinbase or a Bitcoin ATM, and it publishes a Bitcoin address plus a Gmail inbox for payment proof.

We are not repeating that address or that inbox here. A cryptocurrency payment is hard to reverse, and the proof leaves your hands before the upgrade does.

The same page says access is not immediate. One line allows up to 12 to 24 hours.

Another public line, about the hand review, says the upgrade happens within minutes and at most 24 hours.

Plan for the longer one. There is no published refund rule next to either line.

If a member is active and, in the site's words, will "behave rightly," Feetify says it may extend Premium to 24 months at no charge.

That is an offer the site can make. It is not a promise we can audit.

The same $49 crypto offer is published for buyers. We did not find a $4.99 monthly Feetify plan, or a lifetime price on Feetify's own menu.

We also did not find a published commission rate for free sellers, and we are not filling that hole with a percentage from a third-party guide.

A different official page, aimed at FeetFinder, offers $49 for 12 months or $80 lifetime on FeetFinder if you use that page's link.

The page says the two sites are "working together." We quote that wording. We do not treat it as proof that one company owns the other.

Confirm the live FeetFinder checkout yourself, and do not file the $80 figure under Feetify Premium.

Cash awards are advertised for Premium models, including when no buyer has paid.

The public line is a pool of $1,000 to $10,000+ each month, with individual awards described from $4 up to $100 or more.

The large number is a pool, not a typical payout. No award rules or proof sat on the pages we opened, so this stays a claim.

How Seller Payouts Are Described

Premium payouts are described as two routes, and they should be read together.

The direct route is on the Why Feetify page. A Premium seller can give a buyer crypto, PayPal, Cash App, Venmo or other details, and the buyer pays that person.

The site says it does not take a dime, so the seller keeps the whole transfer.

Because Feetify is not holding the money, it is also not standing in a dispute, a refund or a chargeback.

Sharing PayPal or Cash App details can show a legal name, which sits badly next to the anonymous join the site advertises.

Scam-warning copy tells sellers not to hand over full bank details. Follow that part.

The second route is a payment made through Feetify, so the buyer can keep their details off the seller.

The same page says the seller still receives the full amount, and that the site does not take a cut of those payments either.

Order copy says the member is notified within 24 hours or less and that orders are processed by hand.

It still does not say the method, the minimum, the hold or the schedule for getting that money to the seller, or what happens if the file never arrives.

The FAQ tells an unpaid Premium buyer to contact the seller first, then report them.

That is not a payout calendar. We found no seller wallet and no published withdrawal minimum on the pages we opened.

A direct payment you arranged yourself is a different risk from a platform balance. The pattern is spelled out in how to sell feet pics without getting scammed.

Get the money in a form you can verify before you send the file.

Safety, Age Rules and Privacy

The FAQ says you must be at least 18, and that the signup system will not let a younger person join. We did not test that gate with a minor's details, and we would not.

Premium upgrades are reviewed by hand "to weed out scammers and those who are not up to 18 years old." The site does not say which document, if any, that review looks at.

We found no public government-ID rule for every seller or buyer.

Both /privacy-policy/ and /privacy/ returned a 404 on 17 November 2026.

With no privacy policy, the site does not say how long an email, a photo or a payment screenshot is kept after you delete the account.

The FAQ does say deletion is self-serve in settings and that it is permanent, including the pictures and the profile.

Support on the crypto flow is the Gmail inbox for payment proof, plus a contact address on the feetify.com domain. We are not publishing the inbox.

If a payment sits and the upgrade does not, you are writing to a mailbox, not to a named company with a postal address.

Public seller cards on the Sellers Paid page showed places such as the United States, the United Kingdom and France.

That is a sample of profiles, not a list of countries the site says it serves.

A public Trustpilot profile for the domain, captured on 15 and 16 November 2026, was titled Poor at 1.9 out of 5.

The same profile said new reviews were closed because the website had closed, which did not match a live homepage on those days.

Other public pages reported 313 reviews. We did not count them by hand, and we are not using older scores that fight the 1.9 reading.

Seller discussions also include complaints after a cryptocurrency Premium payment: quiet support, no refund, or a thin buyer side.

Those are reports. They are the same shape as the missing paperwork, which is why they count in the score.

What Works and What Does Not

  • Free to join, and some pictures can be posted before you pay.
  • The $49 Premium price for 12 months is on a public page.
  • Account deletion is described as self-serve and permanent.
  • Premium sellers are told they keep the buyer payments the site describes.

What Does Not

  • No company, address or jurisdiction on the pages we opened.
  • The Terms page is marketing copy, and both privacy URLs 404.
  • Premium is $49 in cryptocurrency, reviewed by hand, with proof sent to a published Gmail inbox.
  • The FAQ says most sales happen in messages, and messages need Premium.
  • Payments through the site have no published minimum, hold or timetable.
  • No public government-ID rule for every account.
  • Member totals, paid-seller totals and cash awards stay unverified claims.
  • The Trustpilot profile was Poor, and it said the site had closed while the homepage was up.

The Final Feetify Verdict

Feetify is a real feet site with a Premium product we would not buy as a beginner. The $49 is published. The company, the privacy policy and the platform payout rules are not.

Our score is 3.5 out of 10. Start on a marketplace that shows the fee, the operator and the withdrawal rule before anyone asks for cryptocurrency.

Come back only if you already trust the buyer, you can afford to lose the $49, and you have read the live upgrade page yourself.
