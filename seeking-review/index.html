What Is Seeking?

Seeking is the allowance-minded dating network that used to be Seeking Arrangement. It has operated since 2006.

Charges show on a statement as W8 Tech rather than the brand name, which is the discreet-billing detail members actually use. The company says it has more than 40 million members in over 130 countries.

Treat that as its claim.

You build a profile, answer the questions the site thinks matter, and message inside the limits of your plan. Attractive members can use the site free if the profile is complete and shows a public photo.

Hiding that photo usually means paying for an add-on. Successful members, in the site's own language, are the ones who hit the paid wall when they try to write.

Platinum at $150, Diamond at $375

Seeking raised prices on 1 January 2026 and renamed the old Premium tier. The 30-day Platinum plan is $150, up from $130.

The 90-day plan is $380, up from $330. Diamond, the top tier, is $375 for 30 days, up from $325.

Members who subscribed or renewed before that date keep the old price while the subscription stays active. Let it lapse and the new rates apply.

Prices also move by country, by gender and by promotion, so treat these as the standard rates we could document, not as a coupon. $150 a month is several times a mainstream dating premium. The site is open that the price is a filter.

You are paying for the pool, not for a novel feature.

Diamond is hard to recommend. It more than doubles Platinum, and the messaging and search you actually need are already on Platinum.

The extra is visibility. In a crowded city that might matter.

For almost everyone it is a larger bill for the same conversation.

Plans renew on their own and the card stays on file. There is no refund once you have paid for a stretch, going by the terms.

Members also report being charged again just to reopen old messages after a plan lapses. Set a reminder before the renewal date.

If you still want the history, do not let the plan die and hope the inbox is a souvenir.

On Smart Customer, Seeking held 1.8 stars across 393 reviews when that source was rechecked on 19 October 2026. The repeating complaints are fake profiles, bans with no explanation, support, and the price.
