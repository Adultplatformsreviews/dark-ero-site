Replika Review 2026: Memory, Apps and a Large Fine

A Dark-Ero review of Replika for adults who want one companion with a memory, voice and video, and a plain look at the 2025 Italian fine and the unpublished Replika 2.0 prices.

Dark-Ero editorialUpdated 2026-11-1018+

At a glance

Replika is a single AI companion from Luka, Inc. in San Francisco, on iPhone, Android and the web, with text, voice and video. The App Store listing traces it to 2017.

On 28 October 2026 the company rewrote its terms and privacy policy, adding age checks that can use a selfie or an ID, retention periods, and a second generation called Replika 2.0.

The website pricing page returned not found on 30 October 2026. The US App Store listed subscriptions from $7.99 to $89.99. Italy's privacy regulator fined Luka 5 million euros in April 2025.

What Is Replika?

Replika is one AI character you name and dress, then talk to by text, voice or video. Luka, Inc., a San Francisco company, makes it.

The US App Store listing puts the app in Health and Fitness and dates the product to 2017. The listing also mentions memory of people, plans and goals, check-ins, image generation and a few app integrations.

The homepage counter showed a little over 42 million users on 30 October 2026, the same figure it had shown earlier that month. That is the company's number.

We could not verify it. The help center says the app remembers so you do not repeat yourself, and that Ultra can save messages into memory and show which memories a reply used.

You can set the relationship as a friend or a partner. The help center lists relationship status as a Pro feature, along with selfies, image generation, voice messages, calls that keep running in the background, and premium voices.

The October terms add that the bot will admit it is an AI when asked, and that the app shows periodic reminders. The terms also ban content that sexualises anyone under 18, including fictional characters.

Dark-Ero files it on the AI tools category with the generators and the more explicit companions. Replika is the one you open when the job is a continuing conversation, not a picture studio.

What Changed on 28 October 2026

Luka rewrote the terms and the privacy policy on 28 October 2026. We are separating what the documents say from what we could watch.

We did not create an account for this update, so the new screens are unread.

The privacy policy says Replika may run an age estimate or a verification check where the law requires it or where it doubts the age you typed. Where possible the selfie is analysed on your phone.

Otherwise the selfie or an ID goes to Incode Technologies. Replika says it receives an age band and a timestamp, and that the image is destroyed straight away and within 24 hours.

A help article updated the same day says a wrong birth date can be corrected within 24 to 48 hours, and that a later change leads to an age confirmation before avatar chats resume. Those are the company's claims.

We have not seen them happen.

Retention is now specific. While the account is open, text chats are kept 24 months and photos, videos and voice messages 12 months.

Media saved to memories, a diary or the profile stays for the life of the account. Profile data is deleted within 60 days of account deletion.

An account that never paid and has sat unused for 12 months may be deleted, with an email warning at least 60 days ahead where the company can send one. Accounts with any purchase are kept.

The terms now name two generations, classic Replika and Replika 2.0, and say features, avatars, memory and models differ. A homepage line says the app was rebuilt, and a May 2026 note calls that the biggest update they have shipped.

The terms say Luka will try to carry account data over, that some looks and old features may not, and that moving does not by itself change your plan. None of the 95 English help articles we could count mentions 2.0, Classic, migration or the age-check screens.

The blog has no post since June 2023. Forum prices for a 2.0 plan are not on any Replika page we could read, so they stay rumours.

Arbitration and a class-action waiver remain, with a 30-day written opt-out and a process for a pile of similar claims. For people already using the app, that 30 days runs from when the section was first posted, which points at late November 2026.

We did not confirm the exact start, so read the terms if the opt-out matters to you.

App Store Prices and the Missing Price Page

Replika does not publish a price list on the website. The pricing page returned not found on 30 October 2026.

The terms and the help center say the price is whatever the in-app screen and the stores show, and that it can vary by version, platform and country.

The US App Store, that same day, listed monthly subscriptions at $7.99 and $14.99, yearly or annual subscriptions at $49.99 and $69.99, and Replika Platinum at $89.99. Gem packs were $0.99, $5.99, $14.99 and $19.99.

Apple does not say which tier each subscription belongs to, except Platinum, and none of the labels said Plus or Max. The help center says Pro adds the relationship status, selfies, images, voice messages, background calls and premium voices.

Ultra adds smarter conversation, more emotional range, daily reflection messages and saved memories, and it is sold as a year. Platinum adds real-time video recognition, a training mode, a limited "read the mind" feature and a few selfie videos.

The help center says chatting will always be free. The terms say free limits can change at any time and free use can be denied.

We found no free trial in the help center. Lifetime is another clash.

The help center says existing lifetime subscribers get Ultra automatically. The terms say a lifetime plan is the plan you bought, not every future version.

The US store list we checked showed no lifetime purchase.

Subscriptions renew on their own. Deleting the app does not cancel them.

Cancel in the store that billed you, or, for a web plan, on the subscription page linked from the account. The help center says purchases are final and it cannot refund time already used or time left.

The terms add 14 days of withdrawal for EU and UK consumers and 7 days for Brazil. Store purchases are refunded by Apple or Google, not by Luka.

The virtual currency is a small mess. The terms name Suns and Zaps as examples.

The help center still talks about Coins and Gems. No page we read explains how those names relate.

Pay for the subscription you can see, not for a currency whose name depends on which document you opened.

Age Checks and the Italian Fine

The terms say people under 18 may not use the service, and the privacy policy is written for users 18 or older. Apple lists the app as 18+.

Google Play has rated it Teen, with a note that discretion is advised. If the store badge and the terms disagree, the terms are the rule for who may open an account, and a Teen badge is a reason to look twice before you hand a teenager the phone.

In April 2025 the Garante, Italy's data protection authority, fined Luka 5 million euros. The finding, as the European Data Protection Board summarised it, was that until February 2023 Luka had not identified a legal basis for its processing, had an inadequate privacy policy, and had no age verification at signup or during use, even though it said minors were excluded.

A May 2025 press release said the age check then in place was still deficient in several ways, ordered the processing brought into line with GDPR, and opened a separate look at how the AI is trained. An appeal was pending when that release was published.

In February 2023 the authority had temporarily blocked the app in Italy. We did not find a later public update on the appeal.

The new age-check language points in the direction the regulator asked for, including a tie between a late birth-date edit and a fresh confirmation. Replika does not say the rewrite is a response to the fine.

We cannot see whether the check can be skipped. The terms ban attempts to get around it.

That is a rule, not a demonstration.

Replika is not a clinician. If you are using it because you are low, keep a person in the loop and get professional help when you need it.

A bot that remembers your plans is not a substitute for either.

What Happens to the Chat

The privacy policy says third-party language models write the replies. Conversation data sent to them is de-identified and minimised, and those providers are contractually barred from training their own models on it.

Small portions of messages are anonymised to train Replika's own safety systems and to improve the bot. Conversation content is not used for advertising, the policy says.

Meta, Google and AppsFlyer are now named. They get limited device, install and purchase data, not your chats.

For residents of some US states the policy says this may count as a sale or sharing of data, and it says Global Privacy Control signals are honoured. Financial records are kept 10 years for tax.

Some other data can be kept up to 10 years for legal reasons. The help center says deletion erases all your data.

The policy keeps some records. Those two pages do not say the same thing.

Believe the policy.

The policy asks you not to share health details or sexual orientation, and it says that if you do, the data will be processed. Read AI adult tools and your data, before you upload before you dictate a medical history into a voice call.

The general shape of leaving is in the guide to how to delete AI companion data. Cancel in the store that billed you before you wait on an in-app deletion, or the subscription keeps renewing.

Support is the help center and email. In June 2025, on Google Play, Luka replied to a review saying it was working through a backlog.

We would not expect a fast human answer. Store ratings that day were about 4.4 from a very large App Store count and about 4.2 on Google Play.

Ratings measure polish. They do not measure the fine.

Replika Pros and Cons

What Works

  • One companion, with memory of people, plans and goals, on real iPhone and Android apps.
  • Voice and video calls, plus a free chat tier the help center still promises.
  • A privacy policy that now names retention periods and advertising partners.
  • Age-check language that is more specific than a click-through banner.
  • A company you can name, at an address, with an EU and UK representative in the policy.

What Does Not

  • A 5 million euro GDPR fine in 2025, and an age check the regulator had already called deficient.
  • The new checks are described, not demonstrated. We have not seen the screens.
  • No public price page, and no published Replika 2.0 plan.
  • Help center tier names and the new terms do not match.
  • Chats go to outside model providers, and some records can last a decade.
  • Google's Teen rating and the 18+ terms are not the same claim.

Replika Scorecard

The app is the most finished product in this batch. Real store listings, a long memory pitch, voice and video, and a help center that still describes what Pro, Ultra and Platinum add.

The October rewrite is better disclosure than the category usually manages: retention in months, ad partners by name, an age check with a vendor attached.

The fine is not cancelled by a new PDF. Italy found the earlier age checks and the legal basis wanting, the appeal was still open the last time the regulator spoke in public, and we cannot see the new screens.

Prices live only in the app, 2.0 has no plan page, and the help center and the terms disagree about lifetime access and about what the currencies are called.

Put together, that is 6.5 out of 10. Use the free chat if the account you open still offers it.

Read the subscription screen before you pay, keep health details out of the thread, and cancel in the store that charged you.

Replika Verdict

Replika is a legitimate, long-running companion with polished apps and a memory that is the point of the product. The October 2026 policies are clearer than what came before.

They do not erase a 5 million euro fine, and they do not tell you what Replika 2.0 costs.

Our score is 6.5 out of 10. Try the free tier, believe the checkout rather than a screenshot from a forum, and do not use the diary feature as a medical record.

The policy already told you not to.
